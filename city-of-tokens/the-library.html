What ReLU does in the middle

The expansion layer produces 16,384 raw scores — one per pattern in the catalog. ReLU (max(0, x)) sets all negative scores to zero. Only positive matches survive. This gating is how the Library "selects" — irrelevant patterns are zeroed out, relevant ones pass through. The second layer then reads the surviving activations and writes the retrieved knowledge back into bank's 4,096-dimensional representation.

What the Library is storing

Research (Geva et al., 2021) suggests the FFN stores factual associations as key-value pairs in its weights. The first layer matches patterns (keys), the second reads out payloads (values). Examples of what this looks like in practice:

Pattern: "Rome is the capital of ___" → fires Italy, Europe, ancient, Vatican
Pattern: "riverbank" context → fires erosion, sediment, flood plain, alluvial
Pattern: "financial institution" → fires deposit, loan, FDIC, interest, vault
Pattern: "___'s capital city" → fires geography facts, national information
Attention vs FFN — the division of labor
The square recalculates from scratch every time — it reads the whole sentence and decides what matters, fresh, for this specific input. The Library never recalculates. Its shelves were arranged during training and have not moved since. One process is live and situational. The other is frozen and encyclopedic. You just played both: route first, then retrieve. That division — live routing, frozen retrieval — is what makes the whole system work.

What the FFN does

After the square, each word walks to the Library alone. It checks its current shape against every pattern on the shelves — a space four times larger than the word itself — then walks back out the same size, carrying whatever matched. The expansion is not a detour. It is the search. You need the room to check everything before you collapse back down.

What fires depends entirely on what attention left behind. Attention routes; FFN retrieves.
