The problem
The map gives every token one fixed position — assigned before any sentence is read. Now look at what happens when the same word appears in two completely different sentences.
Sentence A
"The bank was completely flooded."
Position on map: (342, 92)
Nearest neighbor: river, deposit
Sentence B
"The bank was suddenly closed."
Position on map: (342, 92)
Nearest neighbor: river, deposit
Identical. Two sentences with completely different meanings produce the exact same embedding for "bank." The map assigned it one position — and that position is the same no matter what sentence surrounds it.

At this stage, the model has no way to tell these sentences apart. Something has to change. And it does — but not here.

The Map Room

What you discovered
Every token gets one fixed position on this map — assigned by looking up its ID in a matrix trained on billions of documents. Words that appear in similar contexts land near each other. The space has real structure: "unhappy" sits near "happy" but shifted in the direction of negation.

But single-meaning words are the easy case. "bank" has two lives — a river and a vault — and the map gives it one spot, stranded between both families. When "bank" appears in a sentence, the map produces the same starting point every time. The two sentences you just saw were indistinguishable at this stage.
The limitation
This isn't a flaw in the map — it's a fundamental constraint. The map is built once during training and frozen. It can't adapt to context. For the model to know whether "bank" means a river or a vault, something else has to happen after the map assigns the starting position.
To be continued
Further into the city, each token finally meets its neighbors. In a district called the Town Square, "bank" will encounter the other words in its sentence — "flooded" or "closed" — and their presence will pull its representation toward the right meaning.

That's not a metaphor. It's the actual mechanism. The map assigns a starting point. The Town Square moves it.

But first, there's one more thing the city needs to do before it can process the sequence. The travelers have their positions — but the city doesn't yet know what order they arrived in. That's Level 3.
