# Design Constraints

**Before shipping any player-facing change — copy, mechanic, screen, or flow —
verify it violates none of these constraints.** This is a review gate, not
reading material. Run down the list and check each one as a pass/fail test.

These are hard boundaries, not principles to weigh. A change that violates a
constraint fails, even if it's otherwise good. Each constraint is a **rule**
(the pass/fail test) grounded in a **player truth** (the fact about our target
player that makes the rule non-negotiable).

How this differs from `pillars.md`: pillars are **generative** — read them at the
start to derive what to build. Constraints are **evaluative** — run them at the
end to highlight what doesn't belong. Both serve the same player; one creates, one
filters.

---

## Zero Baseline
**Rule:** Never use a term before the mechanic has demonstrated what it means.
The name is the reward for understanding, not the starting point.
**Grounding:** The player knows nothing — not AI, not ML, not the concept being
taught, not technical terminology of any kind.

---

## 3-Second Patience
**Rule:** Every screen must be scannable in under 3 seconds. No single piece of
copy earns more than 2 sentences. If it needs a third, it needs to be a mechanic
instead.
**Grounding:** The player will not read a block of text. If the point isn't made
in one glance, it won't land on first pass.

---

## No Mental Gymnastics
**Rule:** Introduce one concept at a time, and always demonstrate before naming.
If an explanation requires the player to remember something from a previous
screen, it's mistimed.
**Grounding:** The player cannot hold two abstract ideas simultaneously in order
to understand a third.

---

## Play First, Understand Later
**Rule:** The mechanic teaches the concept; text only confirms and names what the
player already felt. Never explain something the player hasn't experienced yet.
**Grounding:** The player learns by doing, not by reading preparation material.
Information front-loaded before the player has experience to attach it to is
wasted.

---

## No Jargon Tax
**Rule:** If a technical term must appear, it arrives last. Pattern: demonstrate
the thing → player experiences it → plain-English description → technical name
(optional). The name is never the starting point.
**Grounding:** The player should never feel stupid for not knowing a term. Jargon
is a wall, not a door.

---

## One Thing at a Time
**Rule:** Do not combine two new concepts in the same round, screen, or feedback
card. If a result screen needs to explain two things, split them across two
rounds.
**Grounding:** The player cannot process multiple new concepts in a single
moment. Each new mechanic, term, or idea needs its own moment of introduction.

---

## Immediate Legibility
**Rule:** The player must know what to do within 3 seconds of seeing any screen —
from the visual design, not from reading instructions. If a playtester asks "what
am I supposed to do here?", the screen has failed.
**Grounding:** The player won't hunt for the next action. The affordances must
answer the question before the player has time to ask it.

---

## Friction is a Bug
**Rule:** Controls, feedback, and layout must be invisible. The only cognitive
load the player carries is the concept being taught — nothing else.
**Grounding:** Any moment where the player has to think about the interface
(rather than the concept) is a defect, not a feature.

---

## Clear Aha Moment
**Rule:** Every level must deliver a clear moment where the concept clicks —
during play or at the end. Tie what the player learned through the experience
back to the technical term that was previously intimidating or unclear, simply
and directly.
**Grounding:** Without a reasonably clear "Aha!" moment, the game is pointless and
the player isn't coming back. The payoff is the concept clicking, not the
mechanic itself.
