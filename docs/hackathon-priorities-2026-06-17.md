## Problem Statement

Non-technical people can't build genuine structural intuition for how transformer/LLM
architecture works from existing (passive) resources. City of Tokens teaches the mechanism
through play — the mechanic *is* the operation. The live risk for Level 1 ("The Gate",
tokenization): the mechanic appears to teach, but a text-heavy presentation blocks
first-pass comprehension. The last week of work went to visual production value (monster /
canvas / 3D / hand-crafted beast), which does not address that blocker.

## Demand Evidence

First real signal, from actual testers (not the founder). Two play sessions logged
(`docs/feedback/level-1/`), plus 2-3 casual. Both testers converge, independently:

- **The mechanic teaches (play-first works).** Ashiya: *"the text was very hard to
  understand what was going on, but once I played the game, I figured it out."* Tavishi
  described tokenization in her own words after two passes: *"words entering an LLM get
  broken into registered fragments before processing."*
- **Text is the blocker.** Ashiya: *"design it to be more game-focused in the UX as
  opposed to have a lot of text."* Tavishi: *"that was a lot of information... I didn't
  read all of it"* / *"too information-dense to absorb while also trying to play."*
- **Differentiation confirmed unprompted.** Ashiya: *"it seems like Duolingo... learning
  is happening via doing."*

This is the first evidence the core thesis (mechanic creates the aha) holds — gated by a
text wall that forces re-reads. It is NOT evidence anyone wants art; no tester asked for it.

## Status Quo

Unchanged from prior design: 3Blue1Brown / Karpathy (passive), papers/blogs (dense,
require ML vocab), build-it-yourself (weeks, needs an engineering background). No resource
teaches the mechanism through doing for this audience.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

Unchanged: curious people who use AI daily and feel they don't understand it, + students in
adjacent fields. The hackathon (2 days out) is a *channel* to test with strangers outside
the founder's network — solving the recruiting problem the prior design flagged as blocking.

## Constraints

- Browser-native vanilla JS, mobile-first, solo builder (unchanged).
- **Hard timebox: ~2 days to the hackathon.**
- **Stated goal: validate key risks via stranger feedback > win the hackathon.** The
  founder explicitly noted these require different activities and chose validation.

## Premises

1. The mechanic teaches; the blocker is **text density**, not lack of art. (Validated by
   two testers' own words.)
2. To validate *teaching*, the instrument must be **frictionless, not beautiful**. The plain
   slash version is the better test instrument — art inflates Sensation ("ooh") and can mask
   whether Discovery (the concept) landed. (Founder agreed.)
3. The hackathon is a **validation lab, not a stage.** Optimize the 2 days for clean stranger
   feedback, not for a winning demo. (Founder's call.)
4. Therefore the **text → visual redesign (Thread 1) is the #1 priority** — ahead of the
   beast/3D/art work, which is parked (it's real art direction, but for *after* the teaching
   works text-light).

## Approaches Considered

Silent-playtest the working slash Level 1, text reduced, with strangers at the hackathon.
Effort S. Isolates the one risk art can't de-risk.

### Approach B: Build + test L3/L4 first (original 2026-06-10 plan)
Build the harder mechanics, test those as the gate. Effort L. Rejected for this window: weeks

### Approach C: A/B the art question (plain vs. beast)
Run both head-to-head to measure whether art helps or hurts comprehension. Effort M. Deferred:
good follow-up, but premature before the text-light mechanic itself passes.

## Recommended Approach

**A ruthless text-reduction pass on the plain slash Level 1, turned into a stranger-feedback
instrument for the hackathon.** Cut the copy that testers choked on; teach through the
visuals/interaction; ship a version a stranger can clear with near-zero reading. Then run
silent playtests with strangers and capture first-pass comprehension. Art stays parked.

## Open Questions

2. **Pass bar for the hackathon:** proposed — of ~8-10 strangers, ≥70% reach the "why there?"

3. Pricing model and second-game timing — still deferred to post-launch (unchanged).

## Success Criteria

1. **Primary (validation):** ≥70% of strangers who play the text-light Level 1 with zero
   instruction reach the aha / describe tokenization in their own words on first pass.
2. **Secondary (diagnosis):** capture where each tester chooses to *read* vs *play* — the
   text-density signal. Fewer read-stops than the current build = the reduction worked.
3. Pass ⇒ play-first thesis validated; proceed to build more (text-light) and re-introduce
   art. Fail ⇒ redesign onboarding/mechanic before scaling, do NOT add levels or art.
